Green · healthy   Amber · watch closely   Red · needs attention. These are playback indicators, not an ISP speed test.

What do these numbers mean?

Buffer ahead: downloaded video ready to play. Under 2 seconds is red, 2–5 seconds amber, and 5+ seconds green while playing. A low buffer increases the risk of a pause.

Chunk download: milliseconds to fetch a piece of video (1,000 ms = 1 second). Green means it downloaded in less than half its playback duration; red means it took longer to download than to watch.

Live delay: seconds behind the provider’s live edge. This stays neutral: a longer delay does not necessarily mean a bad connection and is not stadium-to-screen delay.

Interruptions and request errors: totals for this playback. Colors reflect current buffering or errors in the last 30 seconds, so a recovered stream can return to green. Dropped frames show a recent rendering percentage; above 1% is amber, above 5% red. Startup, pause, and seeking are not low-buffer alerts. These guides cannot identify whether a problem comes from your device, network, or provider.
